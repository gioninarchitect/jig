Core Workflow

Photo Scan & Identify

How TnT-ZA uses your phone camera + Claude AI Vision to identify plants, containers, batches, and scale readings — no dedicated scanner hardware needed.

No special equipment required. Any smartphone with a camera can scan. The system uses AI vision to read tags, labels, and scale displays from photos.
What You Can Scan
🌱

Plant Tags

Reads ZA-XXXXXX identifiers from plant labels, stickers, or handwritten tags.

📦

Container Labels

Identifies BIN-001, RACK-017, BAG-033, JAR-112 from container stickers.

📄

Batch Stickers

Reads B-2026-012 batch numbers from labels on sealed packages.

⚖️

Scale Displays

Reads digital scale displays and extracts the weight in grams or kilograms automatically.

How It Works
1

Open the Scan Page

Tap Scan in the sidebar (or bottom nav on mobile). The scan page loads with a camera button.

2

Open Camera

Tap "Open Camera". Your phone's rear camera activates. Point it at the plant tag, container label, batch sticker, or scale display.

3

Take the Photo

Tap the green capture button. The photo is taken and sent to Claude AI Vision for analysis. You'll see "Analyzing..." for 2-3 seconds.

4

AI Identifies the Item

Claude Vision reads the text in the photo and determines if it's a plant ID, container ID, batch number, or scale reading. It returns a confidence score.

5

Record is Loaded

If matched, the full record loads instantly — strain, phase, zone, last weight, handler. You see the current state of that item without searching.

6

Take Action

Tap "View Details" to see the full timeline, or "Scan Again" to scan the next item. From the detail page you can transition phases, record weights, or trigger handovers.

Scale Reading Workflow

How Scale Photos Work

▼

When you photograph a digital scale:

  • Claude Vision reads the number on the scale display
  • It detects the unit (grams or kilograms)
  • The result shows as "Scale Reading: 652.3g"
  • This is the same technology used in the Origin stocktake app for OCR

Tips for clear scale photos:

  • Ensure the scale display is well-lit (no glare)
  • Hold the phone steady and straight-on (not at an angle)
  • Wait for the scale to stabilize before photographing
  • Include only the scale display in frame — crop out clutter
Why Every Scan Matters
Weight is tracked at every zone transition. When a container moves from Grow Room to Drying Room, it must be weighed. If the weight loss exceeds the threshold (5% for bins/bags/jars, 15% for drying racks), a CRITICAL anomaly fires immediately to all senior staff. This cannot be dismissed — it must be investigated.

The Container Lifecycle (Scan at Every Step)

▼
  • LOAD — Scan container + weigh → product goes into container in Grow Room
  • MOVE — Scan container + weigh → container moves to Drying Room (weight mandatory)
  • HANDOVER — Scan container + weigh → one person hands to another (both recorded)
  • UNLOAD — Scan container + weigh → product comes out (weight compared to last event)

At every step, the photo scan identifies the container and the scale photo captures the weight. The system compares automatically.

What Triggers a Weight Variance Alert?

▼

When a container is unloaded, moved, or handed over, the system checks:

  • Bins, Bags, Jars: >5% weight loss from last recorded weight = ALERT
  • Drying Racks: >15% weight loss = ALERT (natural moisture loss is expected)

When triggered:

  • An Anomaly record is created (type: CONTAINER_WEIGHT_VARIANCE)
  • All senior roles (Super Admin, Tenant Admin, Facility Manager) receive a notification
  • The anomaly appears in the red dashboard panel
  • It cannot be dismissed — must be resolved with investigation notes
Who Uses the Scanner

Cultivator

Scans plants for phase transitions. Scans containers for LOAD/UNLOAD/MOVE events. Most frequent scanner.

💼

Facility Manager

Scans containers at handover points. Records weights. Resolves variance alerts.

🛡

Tips for Best Results

Photo Tips

▼
  • Lighting: Ensure the tag/label is well-lit. Avoid shadows and glare.
  • Distance: Fill about 50% of the frame with the label. Not too far, not too close.
  • Angle: Shoot straight-on, not at an angle. The AI reads text better when flat.
  • Clarity: Hold the phone steady. Blurry photos reduce confidence.
  • One item at a time: Frame one tag/label per photo. Multiple IDs in one photo may confuse.

If the Scan Fails

▼

If the AI returns "Unknown" or low confidence:

  • Try again with better lighting or a closer crop
  • If the label is damaged or unreadable, you can always search manually on the Plants or Containers page
  • Report damaged labels to the Facility Manager for replacement
