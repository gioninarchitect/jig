TICKETS · CORE OF OPERATIONS
Step-by-step wizard for stakeholders
04 May 2026 · v1.0

Tickets are not a bug list. They are how the farm runs.

Every job at ILCO that needs a human to do something becomes a ticket. Daily checks. Cleaning rounds. Equipment calibrations. Mould observations. Driver inspections. Staff requests. RP sign-offs. Owner approvals. SOP executions. SMF reviews. One inbox. One queue. One source of truth for what needs to happen.

This wizard walks you through how a ticket is born, lives, and dies — and why making this the operational substrate (not a side feature) changes how the entire farm operates.

01Why tickets-as-core

Three layers, one substrate. Same model for every event.
Layer 1 · Tickets

What needs to happen

Actionable items with assignee, priority, due time, SLA timer. Includes: SOP execution requests, fault reports, requisitions, RP sign-offs, AR approvals, drift alerts.

Layer 2 · Specialised data

What the work captured

The rich record of what was actually done — IPM observation values, daily check items, calibration readings, harvest yields, COA results. Each domain has its proper data shape.

Layer 3 · AuditLog

What happened, immutably

Hash-chained, append-only, regulator-grade. Every action by every actor — timestamp, who did it, what they did, hash of the previous entry. Tampering breaks the chain.

Why this design wins

Every event in the system — a daily check, a mould sighting, a vaccine due, a calibration overdue, a SAHPRA drift — flows through the same substrate. Staff have one inbox to look at. Auditors have one place to query. Operations leadership has one ticker for facility health. No more paper SOPs sitting in a binder; no more WhatsApp groups for fault reports; no more "where did we capture that".

02The 7-step wizard · how a ticket lives

Walk through this in the meeting if anyone asks "but how does it actually work?"
1

Trigger

Origin of the ticket

Three things can spawn a ticket: (a) a person reports it ("printer broken"), (b) a scheduled SOP fires ("daily check Bay 3 · 06:00"), or (c) the system detects something ("greenhouse added → SMF C.3.1 stale").

System action Ticket row created with priority, category, workflow stage, and either assignedToId (a person) or assignedToRole (a department).
2

Routing

Who sees it

If assigned to a specific person → it appears in their dashboard's "Mine" tab. If assigned to a role → it sits in the role's queue and any qualified staff can claim it. RP-bound items land in Berne's queue. Owner-tier items sit in the Owner Approvals banner. Maintenance items go to Calvin's IT queue if it's IT, or to facility maintenance otherwise.

System action Routed by role + workflow stage. Notification surfaces appear automatically: ApprovalsWaitingBanner, BottleneckRadar, dashboard widget counts.
3

SLA timer kicks in

When it must be acted on

Every ticket has a maximum age before it's "breached". Critical = 4 hours · High = 24 hours · Medium = 3 days · Low = 1 week. The ticket card shows a live age stamp; once the SLA is exceeded, it turns red with a 🔥 flame icon.

System action No reminders, no nagging emails. The visible flame in the dashboard is the prompt.
4

Action — work happens

The actual job

The assignee opens the ticket, does the work in real life, and captures it in the system. For a daily check: tick the items in the daily-check form. For a mould sighting: photograph + select severity + describe. For a calibration: enter the 3 weight readings. The ticket form mirrors the paper SOP exactly.

System action A row is written to the relevant specialised table (DailyCheckRecord, IPMObservation, EquipmentCalibration, etc.) — the rich data lives there. The ticket points to it.
5

Sign-off chain (where applicable)

When regulator-grade evidence is needed

Some tickets need a sign-off chain before they close — RP_SIGNOFF tickets, COMPLIANCE_APPROVAL, DAR review, AR approval. The chain enforces order: RP signs first, DAR signs second, AR signs third. Each signature is timestamped with a real human ID. No skipping, no out-of-order.

System action Ticket status moves through the chain. AR approval freezes a snapshot — for SMF sections, this becomes an SMFSectionVersion row.
6

Resolution

Closing the loop

When the ticket is COMPLETED or CLOSED, the system records resolvedAt and resolvedById — the audit trail knows who closed it. If anyone closes wrongly, a "reopen" path exists with reopenedById + reopenedAt recorded.

System action Closing also clears the ticket from active queues. It still lives in the database — searchable, queryable, never deleted.
7

Audit + reflection

Forever

Every state change of the ticket is hash-chained into the AuditLog. SAHPRA can later ask "show me every ticket that triggered an SMF change in March" — we run one query and produce the entire trail with full provenance: who, when, what data, what signatures.

System action AuditLog entries are append-only and SHA-256 chained. The chain can be verified at any time with verifyHashChain().
