SOP-001 to SOP-003
Daily Operations
Opening, running, and closing the branch. These procedures are performed every trading day without exception.
SOP-001: Branch Opening Procedure
Branch Manager / Senior Staff 15-20 minutes Daily

Purpose: To ensure the branch is properly prepared for trading, all systems are operational, and the opening cash float is verified before the first customer is served.

Procedure

  1. Arrive 30 minutes before trading hours
    Unlock the premises, disarm the alarm, do a visual walk-through for security.
  2. Power on all POS terminals and check internet connectivity
    Verify the POS loads correctly. If offline, the system will operate in offline mode — continue as normal.
  3. Log in to the POS system with your PIN
    Use your personal PIN. Never share PINs or log in as another user.
    POS → Enter PIN → Select "Start Shift"
  4. Count the opening cash float
    Physically count all notes and coins in the till. The standard float is set per branch (typically R500-R1,000). Count every denomination.
  5. Record the float in the POS system
    Enter the exact amount counted. The system compares this to the expected float from the previous close.
    POS → Shift Management → Record Float
  6. Check the product display and restock if needed
    Ensure all products are displayed, priced correctly, and nothing is expired. Restock from the backroom as needed.
  7. Review any overnight orders or notifications
    Check for online orders that need fulfilment, inter-branch transfer requests, or system notifications.
    POS → Dashboard → Notifications
  8. Branch is ready to trade
    Open doors at the scheduled trading time. Not before, not after.
Do Not Skip the Float Count

If the opening float doesn't match the expected amount, record the actual amount and immediately notify the Branch Manager or Owner. Do not adjust the number to match.

Opening Checklist

  • Premises unlocked, alarm disarmed
  • POS terminals powered on and operational
  • Logged in with personal PIN
  • Shift started in the system
  • Cash float counted and recorded
  • Product display checked and restocked
  • Notifications reviewed
  • Branch open at scheduled time
SOP-002: Shift Handover Procedure
All Staff 10 minutes Daily (if applicable)

Purpose: To ensure a clean, accountable handover between shifts. The outgoing staff member closes their session, and the incoming staff member starts fresh with a verified state.

Procedure

  1. Outgoing staff: Complete all pending transactions
    Do not hand over mid-sale. Finish any sale in progress and ensure the receipt is printed/sent.
  2. Outgoing staff: Count the cash in the till
    Count every denomination. Record the total in the system as a mid-shift count.
    POS → Shift Management → Mid-Shift Count
  3. Both staff: Verify the cash together
    The incoming staff member independently counts the cash. Both totals must match. If they don't, recount.
  4. Outgoing staff: End your shift in the system
    POS → Shift Management → End Shift
  5. Incoming staff: Log in with your PIN and start your shift
    Record the opening cash amount (which should match what the outgoing staff counted).
    POS → Enter PIN → Start Shift → Record Float
  6. Verbal briefing: Any issues to know about
    Customer complaints, low stock items, pending orders, system issues — share anything the incoming person needs to know.
Both Staff Are Present for the Count

If there's a cash discrepancy later, both the outgoing and incoming staff are involved. The joint count protects both parties. Never hand over without both people counting.

SOP-003: Branch Closing & Day-End Procedure
Branch Manager / Closing Staff 20-30 minutes Daily

Purpose: To close the trading day with an accurate financial reconciliation, secure the premises, and leave the branch ready for the next trading day.

Procedure

  1. Stop accepting new customers at closing time
    Complete any customer currently being served. Lock the door to prevent new entries.
  2. Complete all pending transactions
    No outstanding sales, layaways, or held orders. Everything must be finalised or voided.
  3. Print or view the day's sales summary
    POS → Reports → Daily Sales Summary
  4. Count all cash in the till
    Count every note and coin denomination. Write down the total before entering it in the system. This prevents "adjusting" to match.
  5. Perform the cashup in the POS system
    Enter the actual cash counted. The system will calculate expected vs actual and show the variance.
    POS → Cashup → Enter Denomination Counts → Submit
  6. Investigate any variance over R20
    If the difference between expected and actual exceeds R20, check for: missed transactions, incorrect change given, refunds not processed, or miscounted cash. Document the finding.
  7. Separate the float from the takings
    Leave the standard float in the till for tomorrow. All excess cash is the day's takings — secure in the safe or prepare for banking.
  8. End your shift in the system
    POS → Shift Management → End Shift
  9. Secure the premises
    Lock the safe, turn off unnecessary equipment, set the alarm, lock all doors.
The Cashup Is NOT Optional

Every trading day ends with a completed cashup. There is no "I'll do it tomorrow" or "it was quiet so I didn't bother." An incomplete cashup means the day's finances are unverified. This is a KPI for the Branch Manager.

Closing Checklist

  • All customers served, door locked
  • All transactions completed or voided
  • Daily sales summary reviewed
  • Cash counted (before entering in system)
  • Cashup submitted in POS
  • Variance investigated (if > R20)
  • Float separated, takings secured
  • Shift ended in system
  • Safe locked, alarm set, premises secured
SOP-004 to SOP-006
POS & Sales Procedures
Processing sales, handling customers, refunds, and loyalty. Every sale goes through the POS — no exceptions.
SOP-004: Processing a Sale
Sales Assistant / Budtender 2-3 minutes per transaction Every Transaction

Purpose: To process every customer transaction accurately through the POS system, ensuring proper payment recording, inventory adjustment, and receipt generation.

Procedure

  1. Greet the customer
    Professional greeting. Ask if they're a returning customer (check loyalty). If medical, verify their Section 21 authorization.
  2. Search or select the customer (if registered)
    Search by name, phone, or email. This links the sale to their profile for loyalty points and purchase history. If walk-in, proceed as guest.
    POS → Customer Search → Select or "Guest Sale"
  3. Add products to the cart
    Search by name, category, or scan barcode. Verify the product and quantity with the customer. The system shows available stock — do not sell more than what's in stock.
    POS → Product Browser → Add to Cart
  4. Review the cart with the customer
    Read back the items and total. Confirm this is what they want. Apply any discounts or loyalty redemptions if applicable.
  5. Select payment method and process
    Cash: Enter the amount tendered, system calculates change. Card: Process through the card terminal, wait for approval. EFT: Confirm payment received.
    POS → Checkout → Select Payment → Complete
  6. Issue the receipt
    Print or send digitally (SMS/email). Every sale gets a receipt. The receipt is proof of the transaction for both the customer and the business.
  7. Thank the customer
    Offer to sign them up for loyalty if they're not registered. Mention any active promotions.
Zero Tolerance: Off-System Sales

Any sale not processed through the POS is considered an unauthorized transaction. This includes: accepting cash without ringing it up, using a personal calculator and a notebook, or giving product on an "IOU" basis. All of these are disciplinary offences.

SOP-005: Refunds & Void Transactions
Branch Manager (approval required) 5 minutes As Needed

Purpose: To handle refunds and voided transactions through the proper approval process, maintaining a clear audit trail.

Refund Procedure

  1. Customer requests a refund
    Ask for the receipt (digital or printed). Verify the original transaction in the POS system.
  2. Verify the refund is eligible
    Check the return policy: product is unopened/sealed, within the return window, and the purchase is confirmed in the system.
  3. Request Branch Manager approval
    Sales Assistants cannot process refunds alone. The Branch Manager must approve the refund in the system.
    POS → Orders → Find Transaction → Request Refund
  4. Branch Manager reviews and approves
    Manager verifies the reason, checks the product, and approves or denies in the system. Reason must be documented.
  5. Process the refund
    Refund in the same payment method as the original purchase. Cash refund = cash out of till. Card refund = reverse on terminal.
  6. Return product to inventory (if applicable)
    If the product is resalable, the system adds it back to stock. If damaged, mark as waste/shrinkage.
Void vs Refund

Void: Cancels a transaction before it's completed (wrong item scanned, customer changed mind before paying). Refund: Reverses a completed transaction (customer returning product after purchase). Both require manager approval and both appear on the daily report.

SOP-006: Customer Registration & Loyalty
Sales Assistant 2 minutes Every New Customer

Purpose: To register new customers in the system for loyalty tracking, purchase history, and compliance (Section 21 patients).

  1. Ask the customer if they'd like to register
    "Would you like to join our loyalty program? You'll earn points on every purchase." For medical patients, registration is required.
  2. Collect their details
    Name, phone number, and email (optional). For Section 21 patients: also their authorization number and prescribing doctor.
    POS → Customers → New Customer
  3. Enter details in the POS system
    Fill in all required fields. Double-check the phone number — it's their primary lookup method.
  4. Link the current sale to the new profile
    They start earning points from this purchase.
SOP-007 to SOP-008
Cash Handling & Reconciliation
Cash is the highest-risk area in branch operations. These procedures exist to protect the business AND the staff.
Why Cash Control Matters

Without proper cash procedures, it's impossible to know if money is missing, miscounted, or stolen. The system removes guesswork: every rand in and out is tracked. Staff who follow these procedures are protected from false accusations. Staff who don't follow them become the first suspect when cash goes missing.

SOP-007: Cash Handling Rules
All Staff Handling Cash Always

Golden Rules of Cash Handling

  1. Never leave the till drawer open unattended
    Open the drawer only during a transaction. Close it immediately after making change.
  2. Count change back to the customer
    Place the notes and coins on the counter and count aloud. This prevents disputes about incorrect change.
  3. Never put personal money in the till or take till money out for personal use
    Not even temporarily. Not even "I'll put it back." This is treated as theft regardless of intent.
  4. Report suspected counterfeit notes immediately
    Do not accept the note. Politely tell the customer and ask for an alternative. Notify the Branch Manager.
  5. Cash drops: move excess cash to the safe regularly
    If the till has more than the safe maximum (set per branch), do a cash drop. Record the drop in the POS.
    POS → Cash In/Out → Cash Drop to Safe
  6. All cash movements through the POS
    Cash in (float top-up), cash out (petty cash), cash drops — everything is recorded. No off-system cash movements.
No IOUs, No Tabs, No "I'll Pay Later"

The system does not support credit sales at branch level. If a customer can't pay, they can't take the product. No staff member has authority to extend credit. This protects staff from being held responsible for unpaid debts.

SOP-008: Daily Cashup & Reconciliation
Branch Manager 10-15 minutes Every Trading Day

Purpose: To verify that the cash in the till matches what the system expects based on the day's transactions. This is the single most important daily control.

Procedure

  1. Open the Cashup module in the POS
    POS → Cashup
  2. Count every denomination physically
    Count and record each denomination separately:
    R200 notes × ___ = R___
    R100 notes × ___ = R___
    R50 notes × ___ = R___
    R20 notes × ___ = R___
    R10 notes × ___ = R___
    R5 coins × ___ = R___
    R2 coins × ___ = R___
    R1 coins × ___ = R___
    Smaller coins × ___ = R___
    Total = R___
  3. Enter the denomination counts in the POS
    The system calculates the total. It also shows what it expected based on: opening float + cash sales − cash refunds − cash drops.
    POS → Cashup → Enter Denominations
  4. Review the variance
    Variance = Actual Cash − Expected Cash
    Positive = more cash than expected (overage)
    Negative = less cash than expected (shortage)
    Target: within R20 (minor rounding from change).
  5. If variance > R20: investigate before submitting
    Check: Were all sales rung up? Any cash drops not recorded? Change given incorrectly? Was there a refund? Document the finding in the notes field.
  6. Submit the cashup
    Once satisfied with the count (recount if needed), submit. This locks the day's financial record. It cannot be edited after submission.
    POS → Cashup → Submit
  7. Escalate persistent variances
    If the same till shows shortages on 3+ consecutive days, report to the Owner and implementation partner for investigation.

Variance Response Guide

Variance Amount Action Escalate To
R0 – R20 Normal rounding. Submit as-is. No escalation
R21 – R100 Recount. Check for unrecorded transactions. Document finding. Branch Manager notes
R101 – R500 Recount. Full transaction audit. Written report required. Owner notification same day
R500+ Do not submit. Recount with witness. Full investigation. Owner + implementation partner immediately
SOP-009 to SOP-011
Stock Management & Receiving
Every product that enters or leaves the branch must be recorded in the system. No exceptions.
SOP-009: Receiving Stock Deliveries
Branch Manager / Inventory Manager 15-30 minutes per delivery Per Delivery

Purpose: To verify that what was ordered matches what was delivered, record it in the system accurately, and update inventory levels.

Procedure

  1. Receive the delivery note from the driver
    Check the delivery note matches a pending purchase order in the system. If there's no matching PO, do not accept the delivery — contact the Inventory Manager.
  2. Open the Stock Receiving module
    POS → Stock Receiving → Select Purchase Order
  3. Count and verify every item physically
    Open every box. Count every unit. Check batch numbers if applicable. Do NOT trust the delivery note quantity — count yourself. Compare physical count to the PO quantity.
  4. Enter received quantities in the system
    For each product: enter the quantity you actually received (not what the PO says, not what the delivery note says — what you physically counted).
    Stock Receiving → Enter Qty per Item → Note discrepancies
  5. Record any discrepancies
    If you received fewer (or more) items than the PO, record the actual amount. The system flags the discrepancy. Take photos of damaged goods.
  6. Sign the delivery note and submit in the system
    Only sign after verification is complete. The system updates inventory levels automatically upon submission.
  7. Store products properly
    Place received stock in the correct storage location. Rotate stock (FIFO — first in, first out). Move older stock to the front.
Never Sign Before Counting

Once you sign the delivery note, you've accepted responsibility for the stated quantities. If there's a shortage and you already signed, the business absorbs the loss. Always count first, sign after.

SOP-010: Inter-Branch Stock Transfers
Inventory Manager / Branch Manager 10-20 minutes As Needed

Purpose: To move stock between branches in a tracked, approved, and verified manner.

Sending Branch Procedure

  1. Create a transfer request in the system
    Select the destination branch, products, and quantities.
    Inventory → Transfers → New Transfer
  2. Wait for approval
    The Inventory Manager or Owner must approve the transfer before stock moves.
  3. Pick and pack the transfer items
    Once approved, physically pick the items. Count them against the transfer document. Pack securely.
  4. Mark as dispatched in the system
    The sending branch's stock levels are reduced at this point.
    Transfers → Mark Dispatched

Receiving Branch Procedure

  1. Receive notification of incoming transfer
    The system notifies you when stock is en route.
  2. Physically count received items
    Same as receiving a supplier delivery — count every item, don't trust the paperwork.
  3. Confirm receipt in the system
    Enter actual quantities received. The system updates your branch inventory. Any discrepancy is flagged.
    Transfers → Confirm Receipt
The Transfer Must Exist in the System

No stock should move between branches without a system-tracked transfer. If someone brings product from another branch without a transfer record, do not accept it — escalate to the Inventory Manager. Untracked movement makes reconciliation impossible.

SOP-011: Stock Discrepancy Investigation
Branch Manager / Inventory Manager Varies When Discrepancy Found

Purpose: To investigate and resolve differences between the system's expected stock levels and the actual physical count.

  1. Identify the discrepancy
    Discrepancies are surfaced by: stocktake results, cashup reconciliation, spot checks, or the system's auto-reconciliation report.
  2. Recount the affected products
    Before assuming the count is wrong, recount. Check all storage locations (shelf, backroom, under counter, display).
  3. Check the transaction history
    Review the product's history in the system: sales, receiving, transfers, returns, waste. Look for: missed scan during sale, receiving not recorded, unrecorded transfer.
    Inventory → Product → Transaction History
  4. Document the finding
    Write up: what product, how many units difference, probable cause (if identified), and recommended action. Submit through the system.
  5. Adjust stock if authorized
    Stock adjustments require Inventory Manager or Owner approval. Adjustments are logged permanently with the reason and approver. This is the last resort, not the first step.
Never Adjust Without Investigation

The correct flow is: identify → recount → investigate → document → adjust (with approval). Never jump straight to adjusting the system to match the physical count without understanding why they don't match.

SOP-012 to SOP-013B
Tiered Stocktake Procedures
Origin operates a three-tier stocktake schedule to protect high-value inventory. Daily cycle counts for flower, pre-rolls & concentrates. Weekly medium-value counts. Monthly full branch counts.
Mandatory Stocktake Schedule
TIER A — DAILY Flower (loose & pre-rolls), Concentrates (dabs, rosin, wax) — counted at close of business every day
TIER B — WEEKLY Edibles, Oils, Vapes — counted every Monday morning before trading
TIER C — MONTHLY Full branch stocktake (all categories including accessories, lifestyle, merch) — first week of each month

Enforcement: Day-end cashup submission is blocked until the daily high-value count is completed and submitted. The system will not let you close the day without counting Tier A stock.

SOP-012A: Daily High-Value Stock Count (Tier A)
Shift Lead / Branch Assistant 15-30 minutes DAILY

Purpose: To count all high-value cannabis products (flower, pre-rolls, concentrates including dabs and rosin) at close of business every day. This is the primary shrinkage prevention control. Day-end cashup is blocked until this count is submitted.

System Enforcement

The POS system will NOT allow day-end cashup submission until the daily high-value count is completed. If you try to submit the cashup without doing the count, you will see a blocking screen directing you to the Stocktake App.

Categories to Count

Flower (Loose) Pre-Rolls Concentrates (Dabs, Rosin, Wax)

Procedure

  1. Open the Stocktake App at close of business
    Do this 30 minutes before end of shift, after the last customer is served. Do not count while customers are in the store.
    Stocktake App → New Session
  2. Select the "Daily High-Value" quick template
    The system will automatically load only Flower, Pre-Rolls and Concentrates. You do not need to count accessories, edibles or other categories.
    New Session → Quick Templates → Daily High-Value
  3. Count every product in the session
    Loose flower: Weigh on the scale, photograph the scale reading, enter the weight.
    Pre-rolls & pre-packs: Count physically, enter the number of units.
    Concentrates: Count units (jars, syringes, cartridges).
    Use Batch Mode for faster entry: tap the list icon to switch to batch entry.
  4. Review variances before submitting
    The app highlights items with variance in red. If any variance exceeds 5% (flower) or any units (pre-rolls), add a note explaining why — breakage, sample given, packaging error, etc.
  5. Submit the count
    Once all items are counted, submit the session. This unlocks the day-end cashup. The branch manager will review the results the next morning.
    Stocktake App → Review → Submit
  6. Proceed with day-end cashup
    Return to the POS and complete the day-end cashup as per SOP-008. The system now allows submission.
Why Daily?

Flower, pre-rolls and concentrates are the highest-value products. Monthly stocktakes only catch theft or errors after weeks of accumulation. Daily counts catch discrepancies within 24 hours — making investigation easier and deterring pilferage. A 10g variance is easy to trace today; after a month it becomes impossible.

Daily High-Value Count Checklist

  • Last customer served, shop floor clear
  • Stocktake App open, "Daily High-Value" template selected
  • All loose flower weighed with scale photo
  • All pre-rolls and pre-packs counted
  • All concentrates counted
  • Variances noted with explanations
  • Session submitted
  • Day-end cashup completed
SOP-012B: Weekly Medium-Value Count (Tier B)
Branch Manager 20-40 minutes WEEKLY

Purpose: To count medium-value product categories once per week (every Monday), supplementing the daily high-value counts and the monthly full stocktake.

Categories to Count

Edibles Oils Vapes

Procedure

  1. Open the Stocktake App on Monday morning before trading
    This should be the first task on Monday, completed before the shop opens.
  2. Select the "Weekly Medium" quick template
    New Session → Quick Templates → Weekly Medium
  3. Count all edibles, oils, and vapes
    These are all unit-counted products. Use Batch Mode for speed. Enter the count for each item.
  4. Submit the count
    Review variances, add notes where needed, and submit.

Weekly Count Checklist

  • Monday morning before trading
  • "Weekly Medium" template selected
  • All edibles counted
  • All oils counted
  • All vapes counted
  • Session submitted
SOP-012: Full Branch Stocktake (Tier C)
Branch Manager + Stocktake Team 2-4 hours (depending on branch size) Monthly

Purpose: To count every product in the branch and compare to the system's expected stock levels. This is the ultimate check on stock accuracy and integrity.

Before the Stocktake

  1. Schedule the stocktake in advance
    Notify all staff at least 1 week in advance. Stocktakes happen before or after trading hours, or on a non-trading day. No counting while selling.
  2. Assign counting zones
    Divide the branch into zones: sales floor, backroom, display cases, safe, etc. Assign 1-2 people per zone. Each person counts their zone only.
  3. Ensure all receiving and transfers are processed
    Any pending stock receiving or transfers must be completed in the system before counting begins. Otherwise, the comparison will be wrong.
  4. Open a stocktake session in the Stocktake App
    Stocktake App → New Session → Full Stocktake

During the Stocktake

  1. Count every item physically
    Pick up, count, record. Do not estimate. Do not skip items. Do not "assume" a sealed box has the right number — open and count. If a product is in multiple locations, count all locations.
  2. Enter counts in the Stocktake App
    Search or scan each product. Enter the physical count. The app does NOT show the expected quantity during counting (blind count) to prevent bias.
    Stocktake App → Search Product → Enter Count
  3. Do not adjust counts to match expectations
    If you count 47, enter 47 — even if the system expects 50. The discrepancy report is the whole point of stocktaking.
  4. Flag damaged, expired, or unknown products
    Mark items that can't be sold in the app: expired, damaged, or products that don't match any catalog entry.

After the Stocktake

  1. Submit the stocktake session
    Review totals before submitting. Once submitted, the system generates a variance report.
    Stocktake App → Review → Submit
  2. Review the variance report
    The system shows: expected vs actual for every product. Focus on items with significant variances (> 5% or > 3 units).
  3. Investigate discrepancies per SOP-011
    Follow the Stock Discrepancy Investigation procedure for all material variances.
  4. Report results to the Owner
    The stocktake summary (total stock value, variance percentage, key discrepancies) is sent to the owner as part of the monthly report.

Full Stocktake Checklist

  • Staff notified 1 week in advance
  • Counting zones assigned
  • All receiving and transfers processed
  • Trading paused during count
  • Stocktake session opened in app
  • All items physically counted (blind count)
  • Damaged/expired items flagged
  • Session reviewed and submitted
  • Variance report reviewed
  • Discrepancies investigated
  • Results reported to Owner
SOP-013: Random Spot Check (Ad-hoc)
Branch Manager / Owner 10-20 minutes AD-HOC

Purpose: Unannounced random checks of specific products to verify stock accuracy and deter theft. Spot checks can be triggered at any time — by the owner, branch manager, or during surprise audits. These supplement the scheduled Tier A/B/C counts.

  1. Select 10-15 products to spot-check
    Focus on: best-sellers, high-value items, products that had previous discrepancies, and random selections. Vary the selection each week.
  2. Open a spot-check session in the Stocktake App
    Stocktake App → New Session → Spot Check
  3. Count the selected products and enter in the app
    Same rules as full stocktake: physical count, blind (don't look at expected first), enter actual.
  4. Submit and review variances
    If any product shows a significant variance, investigate immediately per SOP-011.
The Power of Spot Checks

Random spot checks complement the scheduled daily/weekly/monthly counts. Their power is in unpredictability — staff never know when one is coming, which deters pilferage far more effectively than scheduled counts alone. The owner can trigger a spot check remotely at any time.

SOP-014 to SOP-016
Incident & Exception Handling
Procedures for when things go wrong — system outages, customer complaints, security incidents.
SOP-014: System Outage / Offline Operations
All Staff When System Down

Purpose: To ensure the branch can continue trading during internet outages or system issues, and that all transactions are properly captured when the system recovers.

  1. Check if the POS is in offline mode
    The POS app detects connectivity loss automatically and switches to offline mode. A banner appears at the top of the screen. Sales continue as normal — they are saved locally.
  2. Continue processing sales in offline mode
    Products, prices, and customer data are cached locally. Process sales as normal. The system will sync when connectivity returns.
  3. Card payments: use the card terminal independently
    If the card terminal has its own connectivity, process card payments on the terminal. Record the amount in the POS manually if the POS is fully down.
  4. Report the outage
    WhatsApp/call the implementation partner to report the outage. Note the time it started.
  5. When connectivity returns: verify sync
    The POS auto-syncs queued transactions. Verify that all offline sales appear in the dashboard. If anything is missing, report to the implementation partner.
Offline Mode Is NOT an Excuse to Go Manual

The POS works offline. The Stocktake App works offline. There is no scenario where "the internet is down" means "use paper." The apps save locally and sync later. Continue using the system.

SOP-015: Customer Complaint Handling
All Staff (escalate to Branch Manager) As Needed
  1. Listen fully without interrupting
    Let the customer explain the issue completely. Acknowledge their frustration. Do not argue or become defensive.
  2. Apologize and take ownership
    "I'm sorry you've had this experience. Let me see what I can do to help." You're not admitting fault; you're showing you care.
  3. Assess if you can resolve it immediately
    Wrong product? Exchange it. Overcharged? Process a partial refund (with manager approval per SOP-005). Simple questions? Answer them.
  4. If you can't resolve it: escalate to the Branch Manager
    Do not make promises you can't keep. Say: "Let me get my manager to help you with this." Never tell a customer "there's nothing I can do."
  5. Record the complaint
    Log it in the system with: date, customer name, issue, resolution, and who handled it. This is reviewed in the weekly report.
SOP-016: Security Incident Procedure
All Staff Emergency

Purpose: To handle theft, robbery, break-ins, or any security threat. Staff safety comes first.

During a Security Incident

  1. Your safety comes first
    Do not confront, chase, or physically engage with anyone. No product is worth your safety. Comply with demands if threatened.
  2. Activate the panic button / alert security
    Use the branch's emergency system. Call security or SAPS (10111) as soon as it is safe to do so.
  3. Try to remember details
    Number of people, appearance, direction they left, vehicle if applicable. Do this after they leave — do not draw attention during the incident.

After a Security Incident

  1. Ensure all staff and customers are safe
  2. Call the Branch Owner and implementation partner
  3. Do not touch anything if it's a crime scene
    Preserve evidence for SAPS. Do not clean up, move things, or review camera footage until police arrive.
  4. Conduct an emergency stock count once cleared
    After police have cleared the scene, perform a spot check (SOP-013) to identify what was taken. Compare to the system's current stock levels.
  5. File a complete incident report
    Include: date, time, description, items taken (from stock count), SAPS case number, and witness statements.
