Section 2
Change Management Framework
The Real Problem

Staff don't resist technology — they resist change and loss of control. The old manual system, however inefficient, is familiar and comfortable. Our strategy addresses the emotional resistance, not just the technical rollout.

2.1 Addressing Staff Objections

"The old way works fine"

The old way works — until someone makes a counting mistake, forgets to write something down, or a dispute comes up with no record. The system doesn't replace your skill; it protects you from blame. Every sale, every stock count, every cashup is recorded and timestamped. If there's ever a question, the system has your back.

"It takes too long / slows me down"

Yes, it's slower right now because it's new. After 2 weeks of daily use, you'll be faster on the system than you ever were manually. Average staff proficiency reaches 80% within 10 working days. By week 3, most staff report the system is faster than manual.

"You're just trying to spy on us"

The system tracks transactions, not people. It records what happens at the till, in the stockroom, and in the delivery chain. If you're doing your job well, the system makes that visible. If there's a problem, it helps find the cause instead of blaming the nearest person.

"What if the system goes down?"

The POS and Stocktake apps work offline. If the internet drops, sales and counts are saved locally and sync automatically when the connection returns. We've built the system for South African conditions — load-shedding, intermittent connectivity, all of it.

"Are you trying to replace us?"

No. The system handles paperwork, not customer service. You still greet the customers, advise them, build relationships, and run the branch. Staff who master the system become more valuable, not less.

2.2 Transition Timeline

1

Week 1 — Foundation & Kickoff

Owner signs mandate. Staff meeting to announce the change. System accounts created. One-on-one training sessions (30-60 min per person). Dual-run: system alongside manual processes.

Coaching Only — No Consequences
2

Week 2 — Supervised Operation

System is primary method, manual as backup. Implementation partner on-site or on-call. Daily quick check-ins (5 min). First compliance report generated.

Coaching + Gentle Correction
3

Week 3 — System Primary

Manual processes are the exception. Staff expected to complete all SOPs through the system. Non-compliance documented and reported. Refresher training for anyone struggling.

Compliance Expected
4

Week 4 — Full Enforcement

Manual processes officially retired. All operations through the system. KPI tracking begins formally. Non-compliance triggers escalation. Go-live report to owner.

Full Enforcement — No Manual Fallback

2.3 The "Champion" Strategy

Identify one tech-comfortable staff member per branch. Train them first and deeper. Give them the title of "System Champion". They become the first point of contact for other staff's system questions, help troubleshoot before escalating, and receive a small incentive. Staff trust their peers more than outsiders — a champion saying "it's actually easier" is 10x more convincing than the implementation partner saying the same.

3.1 Sales Assistant / Budtender
Reports to: Branch Manager

The Sales Assistant is the front-line representative of Origin. They process customer transactions, provide product advice, maintain the sales floor, and ensure every interaction is captured accurately in the POS system.

Core Responsibilities

  • Log in to the POS at every shift start using assigned PIN
  • Process all sales through the POS — no manual sales, no off-system transactions
  • Verify customer identity and Section 21 status where required
  • Apply correct payment method and issue system-generated receipt
  • Count physical cash at shift start and end (system-recorded)
  • Report stock discrepancies immediately
  • Participate in stocktake when scheduled using the Stocktake App
  • Upsell loyalty program registration to new customers

3.2 Branch Manager
Reports to: Owner / Operations Director

  • Ensure all staff log in and complete shift close procedures
  • Conduct weekly spot stock counts using Stocktake App
  • Receive stock deliveries using Stock Receiving module
  • Submit daily branch report via the system

Section 4
Accountability & Escalation Framework
Principle: Progressive Discipline

Coaching comes first, warnings second, consequences last. The framework protects good-faith staff and holds accountable those who refuse to adapt.

4.1 Escalation Levels

1
Verbal Coaching
First occurrence or during transition (weeks 1-2). Explain the correct procedure, demonstrate, confirm understanding. Documented informally.
2
Written Notice
Repeated non-compliance after coaching, or same issue 3+ times. Written notice documenting issue, expected behavior, consequence. Staff signs. Copy to owner.
3
Formal Warning
Continued non-compliance after written notice, or a single serious offence. Meeting with owner + manager + implementation partner. Performance improvement plan may be issued.
4
Final Warning / Disciplinary Hearing
Persistent refusal or repeated serious offences. Per company disciplinary procedure and SA labour law. May result in suspension, demotion, or termination.

4.2 Non-Compliance Categories

Serious Offences (Immediate Level 3)

  • Off-system sales — processing a sale without the POS
  • Falsifying stocktake counts — entering system numbers instead of counting
  • Sharing PINs or logging in as another user
  • Deleting or altering transaction records
  • Refusing a direct instruction to use the system (post-transition)

Standard Offences (Start at Level 1)

  • Not logging shift start/end
  • Skipping the cashup procedure
  • Not using Stock Receiving module for deliveries
  • Manual workarounds (paper instead of system)
  • Not reporting stock discrepancies
  • Late stocktake submission

4.3 Compliance Reporting

The implementation partner generates a weekly compliance report covering: system usage metrics, cashup completion, stock accuracy, outstanding issues, and positive highlights. Staff who consistently hit KPIs should be publicly recognized.
