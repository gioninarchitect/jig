Mandate to Implement Origin Digital Operating System

I, ___________________________ (Branch Owner / Director), hereby authorize and mandate the implementation of the Origin Digital Operating System across all branch operations effective ___________________________ (date).

I appoint ___________________________ (Implementation Partner) as the authorized third party responsible for:

  • Full deployment and configuration of the Origin POS, Inventory, Stocktake, and Management systems
  • Training of all branch staff on system procedures and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
  • Enforcement of compliance with digital processes as outlined in this handbook
  • Reporting of non-compliance directly to the branch owner
  • Making system changes and updates as required for operational efficiency

  • All manual processes will be phased out according to the transition timeline in this document
  • Staff compliance with the digital system is mandatory, not optional
  • The Implementation Partner has authority to escalate non-compliance to me for disciplinary action

  • KPIs outlined in this handbook form part of each role's performance assessment
Non-Negotiable:

Once the transition deadline passes, manual processes are no longer accepted. Any staff member who refuses to use the system will be subject to the escalation procedure outlined in the Accountability section.

Why This Mandate Matters

Without a clear, signed mandate from the owner, staff will default to what they know — the old manual system. This document gives the implementation partner the authority to enforce the new system and removes the ambiguity that enables resistance. Staff must understand that this comes from the top, not from an outsider.

What the Owner Commits To

  • Back the mandate publicly — tell your staff in person that this is happening and it is non-negotiable
  • Attend the kickoff meeting — be present when the system is introduced to show your commitment
  • Review weekly reports — the implementation partner will send you compliance reports; read them
  • Act on escalations — when non-compliance is reported, follow through with the consequence
  • Do not override the system — if a staff member asks you to make an exception, redirect them to the process

What the Implementation Partner Commits To

  • Thorough training — every staff member will receive hands-on training before being expected to use the system
  • Patience during the learning curve — mistakes are expected in week 1-2; coaching comes before consequences
  • Clear documentation — all SOPs are available as professional, step-by-step guides accessible from any device
  • Responsive support — questions and issues will be addressed within 24 hours during the transition period
  • Fair reporting — compliance reports will be factual and include context, not just complaints
Strategy
Change Management Framework
A phased approach to transitioning from manual operations to the Origin digital system, designed to minimize resistance and maximize adoption.
The Real Problem

Staff don't resist technology — they resist change and loss of control. The old manual system, however inefficient, is familiar and comfortable. Our strategy addresses the emotional resistance, not just the technical rollout.

Common Staff Objections & How to Address Them

"The old way works fine"
Status quo bias

Response: The old way works — until someone makes a counting mistake, forgets to write something down, or a dispute comes up with no record. The system doesn't replace your skill; it protects you from blame. Every sale, every stock count, every cashup is recorded and timestamped. If there's ever a question, the system has your back.

Key Message:

The system protects you. No more "he said, she said" about stock counts or cash shortages.

"It takes too long / slows me down"
Learning curve frustration

Response: Yes, it's slower right now because it's new. After 2 weeks of daily use, you'll be faster on the system than you ever were manually. Think about learning to drive — it was terrifying at first, but now you don't even think about it. We're in that learning phase. It gets easier every day.

Fact:

Average staff proficiency reaches 80% within 10 working days. By week 3, most staff report the system is faster than manual.

"You're just trying to spy on us"
Trust and surveillance concerns

Response: The system tracks transactions, not people. It records what happens at the till, in the stockroom, and in the delivery chain — the same things a manual ledger would record, but accurately and automatically. The difference is: the manual system relies on memory and trust; the digital system provides proof. If you're doing your job well, the system makes that visible. If there's a problem, it helps find the cause instead of blaming the nearest person.

"What if the system goes down?"
Reliability concerns

Response: The POS and Stocktake apps work offline. If the internet drops, sales and counts are saved locally and sync automatically when the connection returns. There is no scenario where you're stuck waiting for the internet. We've built the system for South African conditions — load-shedding, intermittent connectivity, all of it.

Built for SA:

Offline-first design. POS processes sales, Stocktake saves counts, Dispatch saves deliveries — all without internet. Auto-syncs when back online.

"Are you trying to replace us?"
Job security fears

Response: No. The system handles paperwork, not customer service. You still greet the customers, advise them, build relationships, and run the branch. What changes is that you spend less time on admin and more time on the work that actually matters. The system makes you more effective, not less necessary. Staff who master the system become more valuable, not less.

Transition Timeline

The rollout follows a 4-week phased approach. Each phase builds on the last, and no phase begins until the previous one is stable.

1

Week 1 — Foundation & Kickoff

Owner signs mandate. Staff meeting to announce the change. System accounts created for all staff. One-on-one training sessions begin (30-60 min per person). Staff use the system alongside manual processes (dual-run).

Coaching Only — No Consequences
2

Week 2 — Supervised Operation

Staff use the system as the primary method with manual as backup. Implementation partner on-site or on-call for questions. Daily quick check-ins (5 min) to resolve issues. First compliance report generated for owner.

Coaching + Gentle Correction
3

Week 3 — System Primary

Manual processes are now the exception, not the default. Staff are expected to complete all SOPs through the system. Non-compliance is documented and reported to the owner. Refresher training for anyone still struggling.

Compliance Expected
4

Week 4 — Full Enforcement

Manual processes are officially retired. All operations must go through the system. KPI tracking begins formally. Non-compliance triggers the escalation procedure. Go-live report to owner.

Full Enforcement — No Manual Fallback

The "Champion" Strategy

Identify one staff member per branch who is tech-comfortable and willing. Train them first and deeper. Give them the title of "System Champion". Their role:

  • First point of contact for other staff's system questions
  • Helps troubleshoot before escalating to the implementation partner
  • Gets early access to new features and updates
  • Receives a small incentive (bonus, recognition, or responsibility upgrade)
Why this works:

Staff trust their peers more than outsiders. A champion who says "it's actually easier once you get used to it" is 10x more convincing than the implementation partner saying the same thing.

Quick Wins (Deliver in Week 1)

Show staff immediate benefits to build momentum:

  • Cashup in 5 minutes — show how the system calculates expected vs actual automatically (vs 30 min manual)
  • No more stock arguments — demonstrate the audit trail: who received what, when, signed digitally
  • Customer loyalty lookup — show how points and purchase history appear instantly at POS
  • Shift handover is automatic — no more handwritten shift notes; the system tracks it

Role Definitions & Performance KPIs
Each role has defined responsibilities, system obligations, and measurable KPIs that form part of the performance review.
Sales Assistant / Budtender
Reports to: Branch Manager

Job Description

The Sales Assistant is the front-line representative of Origin. They process customer transactions, provide product advice, maintain the sales floor, and ensure every interaction is captured accurately in the POS system.

Core Responsibilities

  • Log in to the POS system at the start of every shift using your assigned PIN
  • Process all sales through the POS — no manual sales, no off-system transactions
  • Verify customer identity and Section 21 status where required before dispensing
  • Apply the correct payment method (cash, card, EFT) and issue a system-generated receipt
  • Count the physical cash in the till at shift start and shift end (system-recorded float count)
  • Report stock discrepancies immediately (product missing, damaged, or expired)
  • Participate in stocktake when scheduled — use the Stocktake App, not paper
  • Maintain product display and cleanliness of the sales area
  • Upsell loyalty program registration to new customers

Critical Failure:

Branch Manager
Reports to: Owner / Operations Director

Job Description

The Branch Manager is accountable for the daily operations, staff performance, stock integrity, and financial accuracy of their branch. They are the system's primary enforcer on the ground and the owner's representative in daily operations.

Core Responsibilities

  • Open and close the branch using the system's daily open/close procedures
  • Verify the opening float count in the POS system before the first sale
  • Perform or supervise the daily cashup — reconcile expected vs actual cash using the POS report
  • Investigate and document any till variance exceeding R20
  • Approve or escalate refunds and voids processed through the POS
  • Ensure all staff log into the system at shift start and complete shift close procedures
  • Conduct weekly spot stock counts using the Stocktake App
  • Receive stock deliveries using the Stock Receiving module — verify quantities against delivery note
  • Submit the daily branch report via the system (auto-generated, reviewed and confirmed)
  • Escalate system issues to the implementation partner within 2 hours
  • Coach underperforming staff on system usage before escalating

Stocktake Operator
Reports to: Branch Manager / Inventory Manager

Job Description

The Stocktake Operator conducts physical stock counts using the Stocktake App. This may be a dedicated role or assigned to a Sales Assistant or Branch Manager during stocktake periods. The role requires accuracy, honesty, and strict adherence to the counting procedure.

Core Responsibilities

  • Open a stocktake session in the Stocktake App before counting begins
  • Count every item physically — do not estimate, do not assume, do not copy from the system
  • Scan or select each product and enter the actual count in the app
  • Report discrepancies immediately to the Branch Manager — do not adjust counts to match expected
  • Complete the full count within the allocated time window
  • Sign off / submit the stocktake session in the app when complete
  • Never count stock while sales are in progress (pause sales or count before/after trading hours)

Performance KPIs

KPI Target Measured By Frequency
Count completion rate 100% of assigned items counted Stocktake App session Per session
Count accuracy > 98% match on recount Supervisor recount spot-check Per session
Session submitted on time Within scheduled window Stocktake App timestamps Per session
Discrepancies reported All flagged immediately Discrepancy log Per session
Critical Failure:

Adjusting a physical count to match the system's expected number (instead of reporting the actual count) is fraud. It defeats the purpose of stocktaking and will be treated as a serious disciplinary matter.

Dispatch Operator / Packer
Reports to: Branch Manager

Job Description

The Dispatch Operator picks, packs, and dispatches orders for delivery or collection. They use the Dispatch and Packer apps to ensure every order is fulfilled accurately and tracked from start to finish.

Core Responsibilities

  • Pick items exactly as listed in the order — verify product, quantity, and batch
  • Pack items securely and mark the order as packed in the Packer App
  • Confirm dispatch in the system with the delivery method (courier, collection, driver)
  • Record proof of delivery (signature, photo) where applicable
  • Report any stock shortages that prevent order fulfilment immediately

Enforcement
Accountability & Escalation Framework
Clear consequences for non-compliance, applied fairly and consistently. The goal is compliance, not punishment — but there must be consequences.
Principle: Progressive Discipline

Everyone makes mistakes. The escalation framework is designed so that coaching comes first, warnings second, and consequences last. But it also ensures that persistent non-compliance is not tolerated. The framework protects good-faith staff and holds accountable those who refuse to adapt.

Escalation Levels

1
Verbal Coaching
First occurrence or during the transition period (weeks 1-2). The implementation partner or branch manager explains the correct procedure, demonstrates it, and confirms the staff member understands. Documented informally (date, issue, resolution).
2
Written Notice
Repeated non-compliance after coaching, or the same issue occurring 3+ times. A written notice is issued documenting the issue, the expected behavior, and the consequence of continued non-compliance. Staff member signs acknowledgment. Copy to branch owner.
3
Formal Warning
Continued non-compliance after written notice, or a single serious offence (e.g., processing sales off-system, falsifying stocktake counts). Formal warning per the company's disciplinary code. Meeting with branch owner, branch manager, and implementation partner. Performance improvement plan (PIP) may be issued.
4
Final Warning / Disciplinary Hearing
Persistent refusal to comply after formal warning, or repeated serious offences. Handled per the company's disciplinary procedure and South African labour law. May result in suspension, demotion, or termination depending on severity.

What Counts as Non-Compliance

Serious Offences (Immediate Level 3)
Skip straight to formal warning
  • Off-system sales — processing a sale for cash without ringing it through the POS
  • Falsifying stocktake counts — entering system numbers instead of physically counting
  • Sharing PIN / logging in as another user — compromises accountability and audit trail
  • Deleting or altering records — any attempt to modify transaction history or stock records
  • Refusing a direct instruction to use the system — after the transition period ends (Week 4+)
Standard Offences (Start at Level 1)
Coaching first, escalate if repeated
  • Not logging shift start/end — forgetting to clock in/out on the POS
  • Skipping the cashup procedure — closing without completing the cash reconciliation
  • Not using the Stock Receiving module — accepting deliveries without scanning them in
  • Manual workarounds — writing things on paper instead of using the system (during post-transition)
  • Not reporting stock discrepancies — noticing a problem but not flagging it
  • Late stocktake submission — not completing the count in the allocated window
  • Not following the SOP steps — skipping required steps in a procedure

Compliance Reporting

The implementation partner generates a weekly compliance report for the branch owner covering:

  • System usage metrics — who logged in, how many transactions, shift records
  • Cashup completion — which days were completed, which were missed
  • Stock accuracy — variance between expected and actual from spot checks
  • Outstanding issues — coaching notes, written notices, unresolved problems
  • Positive highlights — staff who are excelling, efficiency improvements, wins
Recognize the Good

The compliance report isn't just about catching problems. Staff who consistently hit their KPIs and use the system correctly should be recognized. Public acknowledgment, small rewards, or responsibility upgrades go a long way.

Owner's Role in Enforcement

The enforcement framework only works if the owner backs it. Specifically:

  • Read the weekly report — don't just file it
  • Ask your manager about flagged items — "I see cashup was missed on Tuesday. What happened?"
  • Don't undermine the system — if staff come to you asking for exceptions, redirect them to the process
  • Follow through on escalations — if a formal warning is recommended, issue it
  • Celebrate milestones — "We've had 30 days of 100% cashup completion. Well done, team."
