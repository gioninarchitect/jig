# Section 21 Doctors Course CPD Accreditation Readiness Pack

Version: 2026-05-10  
Course owner: Origin by ILCO Farming Medical Network  
Governance owner: Chief Medical Officer  
Target audience: HPCSA-registered medical practitioners and regional medical practitioners  
Accreditation status: Accreditation-ready draft. Not yet accredited until approved by an HPCSA-recognised accreditor.

## Important Position

In South Africa, doctors usually refer to **CPD points** or **CEUs**, not PDP points. This course has been structured to be suitable for submission to an HPCSA-recognised CPD accreditor, but Origin must not advertise or issue it as an accredited CPD activity until an accreditor has approved the activity and issued the relevant provider/activity details.

## Proposed CPD Classification

Proposed level:
Level 2 short course under 25 hours with a measurable outcome.

Proposed contact time:
Approximately 6.0 to 6.5 hours:

- Module learning time: 339 minutes.
- Final MCQ assessment: 30 to 45 minutes.
- Course evaluation and reflection: 10 to 15 minutes.

Proposed CEU request:

- 5 Clinical CEUs.
- 1 Ethics / Human Rights / Health Law CEU.

Final CEU allocation must be confirmed by the accreditor.

## CPD Quality Requirements Covered

### 1. Relevance to Professional Practice

The course addresses medical practitioner competence in a regulated access pathway for unregistered medicines, with emphasis on patient safety, clinical independence, informed consent, documentation, pharmacovigilance, privacy, and professional governance.

### 2. Adult Learning Design

The course uses:

- Modular learning.
- Case-based examples.
- Explicit learning outcomes.
- Regulatory and ethical standards.
- Knowledge checks through a final multiple-choice assessment.
- A completion certificate for audit and CMO/delegate review.

### 3. Measurable Outcome

The measurable outcome is a formal MCQ assessment with a minimum pass mark of 75%. The assessment includes critical patient-safety, ethics, confidentiality, adverse-event, telehealth, and Section 21 governance questions.

### 4. Ethics, Human Rights, and Health Law Content

The course includes dedicated content on:

- Informed consent.
- Confidentiality and privacy.
- Data minimisation.
- Conflict of interest.
- Telehealth boundaries.
- Patient communication.
- Medical law and Section 21 authorisation boundaries.
- Advertising restrictions for unregistered medicines.

### 5. Evidence and Recordkeeping

For accreditation operation, Origin should retain the following for at least three years, or longer if required by the accreditor:

- Learner name.
- HPCSA number.
- Email address.
- Course start and completion dates.
- Module completion record.
- Assessment score.
- Certificate ID.
- Course evaluation response.
- Accreditation number once issued.
- Any CMO/delegate sign-off status.

The current browser course stores progress locally. For accredited CPD issuing, completion and assessment evidence should be persisted server-side or exported into an auditable register.

## Certificate Requirements

Until accreditation is granted, the certificate must say:

> Course completion certificate only. Pending CMO/delegate sign-off. Not an accredited CPD certificate unless an accreditation number has been issued.

After accreditation, the certificate should include:

- Learner name.
- HPCSA number.
- Course title.
- Completion date.
- CEUs awarded.
- Ethics CEUs where applicable.
- Accredited provider name and number.
- CPD activity accreditation number.
- Certificate ID.
- Assessment score or pass confirmation.

## Accreditation Risk Controls

- Do not market the course as accredited before approval.
- Do not claim guaranteed CEUs before approval.
- Do not advertise unregistered medicines, strains, products, discounts, or therapeutic outcomes.
- Do not issue final network certification based on MCQ pass alone.
- Do not issue CPD certificates without provider/activity accreditation data.
- Keep course content medically educational, evidence-based, and patient-safety focused.

## Course Evaluation Questions

Use the following post-course evaluation:

1. The course learning outcomes were clear.
2. The course content was relevant to my professional practice.
3. The course improved my understanding of Section 21 governance.
4. The course improved my understanding of informed consent and documentation duties.
5. The course improved my understanding of adverse-event escalation and reporting.
6. The course appropriately addressed ethics, privacy, and conflict-of-interest risks.
7. The assessment reflected the course content.
8. I would recommend this course to a colleague involved in Section 21 medical workflows.
9. What should be improved before accreditation or next delivery?
10. What is one change you will make in your practice after completing the course?
